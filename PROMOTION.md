**Title**

```
FIFA's own 2010 technical report called a June/July Qatar World Cup "a potential health risk", rated the bid medium or high risk in eight of nine categories, and the executive committee voted for it 15 days later
```

**Body**
```
FIFA published its bid evaluation on 17 November 2010, before the vote. On
Qatar the medical assessment was explicit:

  "the fact that the competition is planned in June/July, the two hottest
  months of the year in this region, has to be considered as a potential
  health risk for players, spectators, officials and the FIFA family"

The same report rated Qatar medium or high risk in eight of nine operational
categories, and noted the proposed stadium cooling technology did not yet
exist at the scale required. Qatar got the worst overall ranking of the 2022
candidates.

On 2 December 2010 the executive committee awarded it the tournament anyway,
beating the USA 14-8 in the final round. Five years later FIFA moved the
tournament to November/December, disrupting every domestic calendar in Europe.

Blatter, to Swiss television in 2014:

  "Of course, it was a mistake... The technical report indicated clearly that
  it was too hot in summer, but despite that the executive committee decided
  with quite a big majority that the tournament would be in Qatar."

Reuters' contemporaneous report on the evaluation:
https://www.reuters.com/article/us-soccer-fifa-world/qatar-world-cup-a-potential-health-risk-fifa-idUSTRE6AG2JJ20101117/

What gets me is that this isn't a leak or an allegation. FIFA wrote it down,
published it, and then voted against its own assessment.
```

## Handling the obvious pushback

**"This is just a Wikipedia rewrite."**
Fair on some records. The differentiator is the document layer and the evidence
tiers — the Garcia and Eckert wording side by side is not something you get from
a Wikipedia summary. Say so plainly and don't oversell.

**"AI slop."**
Get ahead of it: 10 of 29 images are AI illustrations, badged as such, used only
where no archive photograph exists. The text and citations are not generated —
they're quoted from named documents you can click through to.

**"You're just anti-FIFA."**
The archive marks records DISPUTED where the evidence is contested, includes
denials, and notes that Blatter and Platini were acquitted criminally. Point at
the ALLEGED and OPEN tiers. That's the answer.

**"Some of this is one-sided."**
If someone finds a missing response or denial, add it. Fastest way to build
credibility is to visibly take a correction.
